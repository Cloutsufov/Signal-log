How to read this page — start here
  1. Nothing on this page is advice. Every box is either a measurement or a piece of context. The one box that looks like a recommendation is labelled a logged hypothesis, because that is all it is: a falsifiable guess being written down so it can be scored against reality tomorrow.
  2. 1The call. Direction, confidence, and the reasoning. Read it last, not first.
  3. 2Already priced in. The move the options market is charging for. A call that predicts a move smaller than this is predicting nothing.
  4. 3What it costs. Breakeven and spread. The underlying has to move this far before you are flat, never mind ahead.
  5. 4Volatility context. Whether options are expensive or cheap versus how much this has actually been moving lately.
  6. 5Agreement. Whether the correlated instruments are telling the same story. When they don't, at least one is noise.
  7. 6Track record. How often this has been right so far, and what the options actually did. This is the box that decides how much the others are worth.
  8. 7Markets. Current prices and freshness per symbol.
  9. 8Pipeline health. Whether the machinery ran.

Watching this on another device

This page is a plain URL on GitHub Pages. Open it in any browser — a second phone, a tablet, a desktop next to your broker app — and bookmark it or add it to your home screen. There is no login and nothing to install, because there is nothing here worth protecting: it is public market data and your own logged guesses.

It does not connect to a brokerage account, and it does not need to. Your broker shows you positions; this shows you whether the reasoning has been any good. Keep them side by side and let each do its job.
